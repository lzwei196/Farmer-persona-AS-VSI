## Decision Rules and Typical Practice Ranges: Nigeria

### Fertilizer Use Decision

Who uses inorganic fertilizer and who doesn't:
- ~35% of Nigerian plots receive inorganic fertilizer
- Fertilizer use is highest in the north (Sudan/Guinea Savanna): ~45-55%
  for cereals, driven by government subsidy programs
- Fertilizer use is lowest in the south (Humid Forest): ~15-25%,
  because root crops (cassava, yam) respond less visibly to inorganic N
- Farmers closer to markets (<15 km) are more likely to use fertilizer
- Farmers with formal education are more likely to use fertilizer
- Female-managed plots have ~5-10 percentage points lower fertilizer use
- Farmers who experienced drought shock are slightly LESS likely to invest
  in fertilizer (cash constraint from lost harvest)

### Nitrogen Application Rates (for those who use fertilizer)

| Crop | Typical N rate (kg/ha) | Range | Notes |
|------|----------------------|-------|-------|
| Maize | 40-80 | 20-120 | Higher in Guinea Savanna |
| Sorghum | 30-60 | 15-80 | Often from NPK compound only |
| Millet | 20-40 | 10-60 | Lowest cereal rates |
| Rice | 50-80 | 30-120 | Upland vs. lowland varies |
| Cassava | 0-20 | 0-40 | Most farmers apply zero N |
| Yam | 10-30 | 0-50 | Organic matter preferred |
| Cowpea/beans | 0-15 | 0-30 | N-fixing; little fertilizer |

IMPORTANT: These are per-hectare rates. For a 0.25 ha plot, actual kg
applied would be roughly one-quarter of these values. Many smallholders
apply one or two 50kg bags of NPK regardless of plot size, so small plots
often receive higher effective kg/ha rates than large plots.

### Seed Choice

- Improved maize varieties: adopted by ~35-40% of maize farmers nationally
  (higher in Guinea Savanna ~50%, lower in Humid Forest ~20%)
- Improved rice varieties: ~25-30% adoption
- Improved sorghum/millet: <15% adoption
- Root crop varieties are mostly local; improved cassava ~20% adoption
- Farmers who use fertilizer are 2-3x more likely to also use improved seed
- Seed saving is widespread — many farmers replant from last harvest

### Labor Norms

- Typical family labor: 50-150 person-days per hectare for cereals,
  100-250 for root crops (more labor-intensive)
- Hired labor: ~30% of plots use some hired labor
- Female-managed plots use slightly less hired labor (cost constraint)
- Larger farms (>2 ha) almost always hire some labor
- Peak labor demands: land preparation, weeding, harvest

### Pesticide Use

- ~15% of plots nationally
- Higher for cowpea (~40% — pod borer is a major pest)
- Higher for vegetables and cash crops
- Very low for cassava and yam (<5%)
- Access constraint: many farmers know pesticides would help but can't
  afford them or can't get them locally
