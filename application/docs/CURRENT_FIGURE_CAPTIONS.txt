Fig. 1. Study design, validation and nitrogen-input scenarios. (a) LSMS–ISA surveys provided 490 plot profiles from seven countries, with 70 profiles per country. (b) Three LLM interfaces generated decisions for the true-country benchmark and for a paired experiment in which seven country labels were assigned to 70 fixed Tanzanian profiles. (c) Validation compared predictions with individual decisions, country-level targets and observed trait associations. Plot areas and post-stratification weights were used to estimate nitrogen quantities for eligible survey records. (d) Checks included transfer benchmarks, paired label contrasts, bootstrap and exact permutation tests, and sensitivity analyses. Country codes: ETH, Ethiopia; MWI, Malawi; MLI, Mali; NER, Niger; NGA, Nigeria; TZA, Tanzania; UGA, Uganda.

Fig. 2. Fertilizer-use targets, LLM predictions and the national-statistics benchmark. Open circles show survey-weighted targets; orange points show post-stratified ensemble predictions for (a) fertilizer adoption, (b) nitrogen rate and (c) nitrogen quantity per eligible plot. Orange error bars show 95% profile-bootstrap confidence intervals with post-stratification weights held fixed. Purple diamonds in (a,b) show leave-one-country-out predictions from a regression of the survey target on national fertilizer use per hectare of arable land (FAO data, World Bank indicator AG.CON.FERT.ZS). MAE is the mean absolute error across the seven countries. Grey ticks in (b,c) show targets obtained by capping plot rates above 300 kg N ha⁻¹ rather than excluding them. †Adoption targets for Ethiopia and Niger differ in definition from those for the other countries (Section 2.5). Shading highlights Tanzania.

Fig. 3. Agreement between predicted and observed fertilizer use across seven countries. (a) Within-country Pearson correlations between predicted and observed nitrogen rates for the LLM ensemble and matched- and rich-input conventional baselines. (b) The same correlations plotted against unweighted country-wave fertilizer-use prevalence. (c) Post-stratified ensemble nitrogen-quantity errors, calculated as predicted minus observed quantity per eligible plot; orange denotes Tanzania. Error bars in (a,c) show 95% bootstrap confidence intervals. Supplementary Table S7 reports paired differences between the correlations.

Fig. 4. Country-label effects on predictions and individual agreement. Results use 70 fixed Tanzanian profiles. (a) Mean predicted nitrogen rates plotted against unweighted fertilizer-use prevalence in the countries named by the labels. (b) Shares of total prediction variance attributable to country-label and profile main effects; other components are not shown. (c,d) Higher-use (Ethiopia, Malawi, Nigeria) minus lower-use (Niger, Tanzania, Uganda) contrasts in nitrogen rate (c) and mean label-specific Pearson correlation with observed rates (d). Mali is excluded from these contrasts. Error bars in (b–d) show 95% confidence intervals from profile bootstraps stratified by observed adoption.

Fig. 5. Predicted nitrogen rates and individual agreement under each country label. (a) Unweighted fertilizer-use prevalence in the countries named by the labels (top) and mean predicted nitrogen rates for 70 Tanzanian profiles (heatmap). (b) Pearson correlations with observed Tanzanian nitrogen rates. Ensemble predictions are the mean of the three interfaces’ predictions.

Fig. 6. Observed and predicted associations of farmer and plot characteristics with fertilizer use. Panels (a,b) show coefficients from regressions of fertilizer adoption (a) and nitrogen rate (b) on eight characteristics, with country fixed effects. Models used LLM ensemble predictions for 488 profiles (orange) and survey-weighted records for 64,917 plots (adoption) and 63,099 plots (nitrogen rate) (open circles). Error bars show 95% intervals from within-country profile bootstraps for predictions and within-country cluster bootstraps for surveys, grouping by enumeration area where known and by household otherwise (Supplementary Text S4). Grey diamonds in (b) show survey coefficients when plot rates above 300 kg N ha⁻¹ are capped rather than excluded. Plot and farm size coefficients refer to a doubling; age coefficients refer to 10 years. Panel (c) shows the out-of-sample share of within-country variation explained by the characteristics, relative to country means. Adoption coefficients refer to observed binary adoption or mean predicted adoption across three providers, not calibrated individual probabilities. The balanced profile sample and survey records have different sampling distributions.

Fig. 7. Observed nitrogen rates and predictions under seven country labels. Results use ensemble predictions for 70 Tanzanian profiles. (a) Observed rates and prediction heatmaps use the same profile order, ranked by observed rate. The observed-rate axis uses a symmetric logarithmic scale with a linear region near zero. (b) Pearson correlations with observed rates (blue, negative; orange, positive). (c) Prediction distributions, with Tanzania shown in orange. Boxes show interquartile ranges, central lines mark medians, and whiskers extend to the most extreme values within 1.5 interquartile ranges of the box edges. Outliers are not displayed.

Fig. 8. Country-label differences in nitrogen rates and expanded quantities. Ensemble contrasts compare higher-use (Ethiopia, Malawi, Nigeria) with lower-use (Niger, Tanzania, Uganda) labels. (a) Cumulative contributions to the expanded contrast, ranked from largest to smallest. Each contribution is the paired rate difference multiplied by plot area and post-stratification weight. (b) Rate differences grouped by observed adoption; point size scales with the square root of post-stratification weight. Shading shows distributions, black segments show interquartile ranges, and ticks mark medians. (c) Contributions by plot-area quartile (Q1, smallest; Q4, largest). Expanded quantities are scenarios for eligible weighted plot records; percentages are rounded.

Fig. S1. Sensitivity of expanded nitrogen contrasts to profile omission and weighting. Results use the ensemble higher-use (Ethiopia, Malawi, Nigeria) minus lower-use (Niger, Tanzania, Uganda) nitrogen-quantity contrast. (a) Expanded estimates after omitting each Tanzanian profile and recalculating weights; the orange line marks the full-sample estimate. (b) 95% confidence intervals from 10,000 bootstrap draws using three procedures: primary adoption-stratified resampling with fixed weights, joint-post-stratum resampling with fixed weights, and joint-post-stratum resampling with weights recalculated. (c) Cumulative net contributions, ranked from largest to smallest, compared with an equal-share reference.
